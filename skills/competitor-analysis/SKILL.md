# Competitor analysis

## 1. Pin down the product
Product, target customer, market/country and price range. Ask if missing; the competitor set depends on all four.

## 2. Pick competitors
- Direct: same customer, same job, similar price. Up to 6; fewer is fine if the market is small.
- Alternatives: what customers use instead (spreadsheets, an agency, hiring someone, doing nothing). At least one.
- Include competitors the user names even if you would not pick them.
- Without web access: list only companies you are confident exist, mark the whole analysis "from model knowledge, verify before use", and leave prices blank. Never invent a company, price or feature.

## 3. Gather per competitor (with source URL and date for each)
- Who they sell to (from their homepage, case studies, customer logos)
- Pricing: plan names, entry price, billing unit (per seat, per usage), free tier/trial. "Contact sales" is a valid answer.
- The 3 things they lead with on the homepage (their claimed positioning)
- Customer voice: recurring praise and complaints from review sites (G2, Capterra, Trustpilot, app stores), Reddit or forums. Only patterns seen in several reviews, with one short example quote and link.
Not found -> `n/a`, never a guess.

## 5. Gaps (each backed by evidence from step 3)
- Complaints that recur across competitors and nobody fixes.
- Customer segments served badly (too expensive, too complex, wrong language or region).
- Price points nobody covers.
Separate evidence from your inference.

## 6. Recommendation
- One positioning statement: "For [customer] who [need], [product] is the [category] that [difference], unlike [main alternative]." The difference must come from a gap in step 5.
- The 2-3 risks: which competitor could copy it fastest and why.

## Rules
- Date the analysis at the top; prices and plans change.
- Do not copy competitors' marketing claims as facts ("the #1 platform"); attribute them.
- Keep the user's product row honest: fill it from what the user told you, not from assumptions.
